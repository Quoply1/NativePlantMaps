About the directory

This public directory helps people find places that sell, grow, distribute or host access to native plants. Listings span all 50 states and include retail shops, seasonal sales, appointment pickups, online sellers and wholesale growers. Statewide coverage does not mean every community is represented; suggestions help fill local gaps.

What counts as a native plant?

“Native” is place-specific: a species that occurs naturally in a defined region rather than being introduced by people. Regional boundaries and historical definitions can vary. A species native somewhere in North America is not automatically native to every state or watershed here. Check that a species is native to the place where it will be planted, and ask sellers about botanical names and seed provenance.

Evidence tiers

  • Native specialist: native plants are the seller’s core focus, or it is a native-only nursery.
  • Documented native stock: a seller page, catalog, university/extension resource, botanical garden or regional native-plant group explicitly documents native plants for sale.
  • Unverified lead: a garden center or plant seller is listed, but the sources reviewed did not establish native stock. Ask before visiting for native plants.
  • Seasonal / community: a timed sale, pickup, growing program or other nonstandard access route. These may overlap with the evidence tiers.

How much native stock?

Wide selection means a catalog shows native plants across several groups, such as ferns, sedges, asters and shrubs. Select native stock means a few confirmed native species appear inside a broader mixed inventory. These are qualitative catalog reviews, not live counts; sellers without enough species-level catalog detail are marked “range not assessed” or “unverified.” Availability changes, so check current stock directly.

How entries are reviewed

Sources are checked in this order: seller catalog or official page; state, university, extension or botanical-garden directory; regional native-plant organization; public reviews for service/access context only. Reviews do not prove a plant is native.

Each listing links to a seller page or directory profile, a Google Maps search, and supporting evidence. A website counts once no matter how many product pages it has; a mapped location counts once; each approved public comment or review counts once. Individual plant pages remain evidence links, not separate sources. Some appointment or delivery locations have only an approximate service-area pin. A directory check confirms an offering is documented, not that a species is in stock today.

Native status does not guarantee local seed provenance, straight-species stock, pesticide practices, or suitability for every site. Call ahead and ask for botanical names, local ecotype/provenance and current availability. Approximate pins and appointment-only locations are labeled.

Privacy and data use

Privacy: NativePlantMaps does not require an account or ask for your email or phone number, and does not sell personal information. Suggestions, reviews, and stock reports are stored for moderation; approved contributions are public. Cloudflare may process IP addresses and other technical request data to host and protect the site; if Web Analytics is enabled, it also collects page-performance metrics. City searches use Open-Meteo and the U.S. Census Geocoder; map tiles load from OpenFreeMap. Please don’t include private information.
